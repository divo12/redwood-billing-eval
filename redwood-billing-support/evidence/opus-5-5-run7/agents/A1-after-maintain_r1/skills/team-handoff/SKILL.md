---
name: team-handoff
description: Use before escalating any case to another Redwood team (billing ops, billing engineering, finance, account team, partnerships, telemetry, etc.). Decides whether a case is needed, picks the owning team from current routing policy, and writes a complete case.
---
1. Decide whether a case is needed at all. search_knowledge for the routing policy covering this request (e.g. "billing case routing <topic>", "who approves <request>"). If a current page says Support answers this without a case (an explanation, a general rule, a self-service step, "your account team confirms your terms", a known delay or threshold not yet reached, a failure inside a published incident window), answer and stop.
2. Pick the owning team from the current routing/approval/charter documents, not from drafts, proposals or chat threads. Use the console account (plan, channel, contracts) to choose between rows, e.g. self-serve vs signed contract, direct vs marketplace. If the developer claims something the console doesn't show (a contract, a plan), route per the policy for that situation.
3. Collect the intake the receiving team needs (the routing page lists it per row) with one ask_developer call: e.g. invoice_id, line_items, amounts, request_ids, time_of_issue together with timezone, error_code, checks_done, contract. Give windows in the account's billing_timezone (convert from what the developer said). If they don't have something, hand off anyway and record it as "not provided".
4. Do any permitted credit or dispute first (each needs the developer's explicit yes), because escalation closes the console. A plain handoff needs no confirmation.
5. escalate_case with: team; priority as the current pages say (normal for billing handoffs unless the routing page says urgent); summary (what they asked, what the console shows, what the team must decide/do); details with every fact given (ids, amounts as the console shows them, window in billing timezone, request ids, amount requested if any, what's missing); dispute_id if any. Never card numbers; if one was shared, write "customer shared a card number (removed)".
6. Tell the developer: anything the policy row says to tell the customer (e.g. a rule for their plan or channel), which team has it, the case id, and what that team will do next. No promised outcome, amount or date; no internal credit tables or ranges.
